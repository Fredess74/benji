# Sustainability and clean-energy impact

Benji supports the challenge by helping users choose and carry out an affordable building improvement. It has not demonstrated field savings or avoided emissions.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Evidence and feasible options"] --> B["Assessment, quote or operating action"]
    B --> C["Completed improvement"]
    C --> D["Energy and emissions outcomes"]
    D --> E["Review comfort, cost and other changes"]
```

Every arrow is a hypothesis to test.

## Mechanisms

**Sustainable consumption:** investigate unnecessary loads, schedules, lighting and heat loss while protecting essential equipment and comfort. Do not treat lower consumption caused by unsafe temperatures as success.

**Modernization:** sequence investigations, approvals, quotes and eligible assistance. Consider maintenance and envelope work alongside replacement. Avoid double-counting overlapping measures. Embodied carbon and lifecycle replacement impacts are not currently calculated.

**Electrification:** screen heat pumps using explicit performance, efficiency, tariffs and emissions factors. Lower fossil use does not guarantee a smaller bill. Missing performance or building information remains a gap.

**Clean-energy access:** prepare questions and records for solar and other assessments. There is no PV-production model, battery dispatch or equipment control in the current product.

**Follow-through:** preserve the estimate, completion date and later bills. Distinguish descriptive changes from savings attributable to the intervention.

**Program access and usefulness:** help identify the appropriate residential/business/provider route, understand current requirements, prepare evidence and follow up on the next step. Measure wrong-route referrals, time to a correct checklist, verified assessment requests, complete applications and projects that actually proceed. Program approval and attribution remain with the operator/evaluator. More recommendations alone are not evidence of better program effectiveness.

## What to measure

| Stage | Metric | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | Time to a correct next step; understanding cash and approval needs | Same-case comparison with existing assistance |
| Action | Assessment/quote request, approval, completed task | Dated confirmation or supporting document |
| Energy | Change by fuel and comparable billing period | Meter coverage, dates, completed-work record |
| Affordability | Actual running costs and initial payment | Tariffs/invoices; price changes separated from usage |
| Climate | Estimated operational kg CO2e difference | Factor, year, energy inputs and accounting boundary |
| Guardrails | Comfort complaints and decision-changing errors | Follow-up and case review |

Weather, occupancy, operating hours, other upgrades and estimated reads confound before/after comparisons. Historical BERDO emission factors are not marginal avoided-emissions factors. The follow-up skill prompts for these limits; it is not a causal evaluation engine.

## First pilot

Recruit 12–20 participants from one segment facing an actual decision. Compare normal assistance with Benji on matched tasks, then follow up on assessment/quote requests and completed work. Measure effort, correctness, trust and willingness to continue.

Revise the approach if it does not reduce effort, produces decision-changing errors, or cannot address the real funding/authority bottleneck. Scale with local data/program adapters only after usefulness is demonstrated.
