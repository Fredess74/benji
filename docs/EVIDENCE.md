# Problem evidence

Hackachusetts 2026 · Track 3 — Climate Tech · Cleaner, more affordable buildings. Sources checked 2026-10-24.

**Problem statement:** people can struggle to turn building-energy information and available assistance into an affordable, authorized improvement. Research supports the problem; it does not establish demand for a paid Benji product or its effectiveness.

## Evidence becomes design

| Barrier | Benji response | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Unexplained bills | Separate usage, prices, billing periods and hypotheses | A bill cannot diagnose one appliance |
| Initial cash gap | Separate upfront payment, later support and operating cost | Benji does not finance work |
| Tenant/owner split | Record payer, beneficiary and approver | A plan does not change a lease |
| Unclear program pathway | Conditions, missing evidence and next contact | Operators decide eligibility |
| Limited time | Short conversations and one next action | Reduced effort remains a user-test hypothesis |
| Lost follow-through | Shared record, owners and task states | An assignment does not launch an agent |

## Existing alternatives

Mass Save already provides assessments and support; [ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager](https://www.energystar.gov/buildings/benchmark) supports benchmarking; Boston offers [decarbonization advisory support](https://www.boston.gov/departments/environment/building-decarbonization-advisor-program). Professionals supply on-site expertise. A spreadsheet is also a legitimate baseline.

Benji's proposed contribution is less work assembling evidence and carrying decisions between these services. A pilot should compare the existing free path and a general assistant against Benji on the same cases. Measure correct next steps, time, cash/eligibility mistakes, actual assessment or quote requests and trust. Willingness to pay, retention and incremental energy savings remain unknown.
